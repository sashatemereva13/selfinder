# Jess — the one person Selfinder is built for

Selfinder is designed for one person, in detail, rather than for a group
(Brian Chesky: "if you pick one person, study them, and take their journey,
you can actually build something really amazing"). That person is Jess.
When a feature, screen, or line of copy is in doubt, ask: does Jess need
this, in the moment they reach for their phone?

Jess is drawn from the founder's own experience, on purpose — the most
successful products in this category began as something the founder built
for themselves (Stoic began as one person's mood spreadsheet). What's
unusual about the founder is the language they use for it; the problem
underneath is common. Jess carries the problem, not the cosmology.

`docs/pitch.md`'s "Who this is really for, at the root" stays true as the
reason the product matters — anyone with an inner world. Jess is the
beachhead: who it's built for first, and who decides what gets built.

---

## Who they are

Late 20s. Ambitious — many plans, expensive taste, and a real belief that
life bends when you push in the right place at the right moment. The
question they live with isn't *whether* anything is possible, it's *when and
where* — how to know where to apply force. Their answer: you feel it.

They're deeply self-aware and reflect at a depth they can't fully put into
words. They trust feeling over reasoning: a solution that's logically right
but doesn't *feel* right won't work for them, however good it is.

They're certain the answers they're looking for are already inside them.
Advice from other people is only useful as a mirror — a way to notice
whether something feels right or doesn't. They don't want to be told; they
want to recognise.

They talk about deep things with a small, close circle — their mum, their
sister, their brother, their best friend — the people who let them see and
know themselves. With everyone else they keep their guard up. They can't explain why;
it just feels right.

## What they want

A life full of meaning, energy, happiness, and power — and to achieve their
ambitions along the way. Achievement matters, but it's not the whole point.

## What gets in the way

**Their own state.** They can tell when they're near love: their head goes quiet,
something deeper speaks, they decide brilliantly and things fall into
place. Away from it, they can't see clearly. In anger — the state they've
landed on most these past weeks — they have a great deal of energy and
nowhere to put it: chaotic, wasted, or turned inward.

It isn't one particular state. Like anyone, Jess can land anywhere on the
17 levels from week to week. The problem doesn't depend on which state —
it's that their clear judgment depends on state, and in the moment they often
don't know which state they're in.

> They already know what to do. They just can't reach that knowing from
> where they are right now.

## When they need Selfinder

Random moments, any time of day. Their head gets loud with overthinking; they
don't know how they feel, or what they should be doing next. Several times
a day in a hard stretch, a few times a week in an easier one.

Because they use the practice to see clearly and act, not only to recover,
they keep coming back when life is going *well* — that's when their ambitions
are moving fastest. (Most journaling apps lose people exactly then.)

## What they've tried, and why it didn't hold

- **Therapy.** They know what a therapist wants to hear and give it to
  them. By the third session they've convinced the therapist they're fine and it's
  solved. The deep things they won't take to someone who might not get it.
- **Advice.** Sensible solutions that don't *feel* right don't work for
  them.
- **Thinking harder.** That *is* the loud head. More thinking, louder.
- **Their close circle** works — and stays. Selfinder isn't instead of them.
  It's for the moments they aren't there, and for what isn't ready to be
  said even to them yet.

## How they actually get clear

Words first, then clarity. When their head is full they need to pour out
what's been bottled, and only then do they start to see. Then they can tell
where they are, and choose what to do with it.

## The job

> When my head gets loud and I can't tell what I feel, let me pour it out,
> see where I am, and get to where I can see clearly — with nobody to
> perform for — so I can act on what I already know.

## How Selfinder should feel to them

A safe space they can reach anytime, anywhere — because they always have
themselves. The "cosy evening by the fireplace" is a feeling, not a time of
day: it has to be just as true on a bus at 10:00 as on the sofa at 23:00.

## What this means for the product

- **A mirror, never advice.** Jess's own sentence — the answers are already
  inside, other people are only a mirror — is the same as Selfinder's
  deepest rule (`RULES.md`, "The answers are already inside the person").
  The persona and the product principle are one sentence.
- **Nobody to perform for.** Jess can talk a therapist into "I'm fine" but
  can't talk a reading into it if no one is watching. Measure stays private
  — no sharing, no score, no audience. And Socrates is the one person Jess
  can't persuade they're fine: he just asks again.
- **Release, then clarity.** Spill (and Guide) aren't extras for Jess —
  they're how Jess clears enough to see. Spill's no-one-watching design is
  exactly their guard.
- **Two equal doors.** Jess knows love is where they see clearly, and also knows anger has power they can use. "Use it / Shift toward love"
  (`docs/shift-concept.md`) is Jess, word for word. Jess ranks love first for
  themselves — and that's their choice to make, which is why the app never makes
  it for anyone.
- **Being known over time.** Jess's close circle helps because they know
  Jess's history. The in-app equivalent is Your Arc: the record of where they've
  been is what lets the app feel like it knows Jess without ever claiming
  anything about them.
- **Anytime, anywhere.** The path from loud head to one clear next step has
  to work in a couple of minutes, mid-day, without needing a free evening.
